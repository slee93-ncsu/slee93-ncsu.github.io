Biography

I am a Ph.D. student in Design (Building Energy Systems) at North Carolina State University, advised by Prof. Soolyeon Cho, and an M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering. I am currently a Graduate Intern with the Grid Automation and Controls Group at the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR).

My research studies how buildings and electric grids interact under normal, extreme, and recovery conditions. I integrate building and distribution-system models through co-simulation and develop data-driven methods for forecasting weather-induced power outages, understanding restoration dynamics, and identifying heat-pump loads at the distribution-feeder level.

Before my Ph.D., I worked as a researcher at the Korea Research Institute of Eco-Environmental Architecture and the Korea Institute of Energy Research. There I led energy performance assessments of buildings and contributed to national research on zero-energy building policy and geothermal heat pump design.

Research Interests

Building–Grid Resilience

  • Outage Forecasting: Forecasting rare, large-scale weather-induced power outages with deep time-series models.
  • Restoration Dynamics: Co-simulating buildings and distribution grids to study recovery, thermal behavior, and cold load pickup.

Machine Learning for Energy Systems

  • Time-Series Analytics: Data-driven modeling for power systems and building energy applications.
  • Grid-Interactive Buildings: Learning-based modeling and control of building loads and HVAC systems.

Building Electrification

  • Heat-Pump Load Identification: Identifying heat-pump loads at the distribution-feeder level from electricity and weather data.
  • Distribution Planning: Load profiles for integration into transmission and distribution system models.
