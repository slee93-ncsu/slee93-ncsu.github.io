Biography

At North Carolina State University, I am pursuing a Ph.D. in Design (Building Energy Systems), advised by Prof. Soolyeon Cho, and concurrently an M.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering. I am also a Graduate Intern in the Grid Automation and Controls Group at the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR).

My research focuses on how buildings and electric grids interact during extreme weather: forecasting weather-induced power outages, modeling building–grid restoration, and identifying heat-pump loads at the distribution-feeder level.

Previously, I led building energy performance assessments as a researcher at the Korea Research Institute of Eco-Environmental Architecture and the Korea Institute of Energy Research.

Research Interests

Building–Grid Restoration

  • Restoration as a System Trajectory: How restoration is modeled, evaluated, and used in decisions across 108 studies.
  • House- and Feeder-Level Recovery: Co-simulating buildings and distribution grids to study thermal recovery and cold load pickup.

Power Outage Prediction

  • Rare, Large-Scale Outages: Forecasting weather-induced outages with Temporal Fusion Transformers and quantile forecasting.
  • Multi-County Learning: When pooling data across counties helps, and when it hurts.

Data-Driven Building Energy Systems

  • Heat-Pump Identification: Identifying heat-pump loads at the distribution-feeder level.
  • Data Center Thermal Analysis: Where GPU nodes run warm and how their temperature changes.
