Data Center Thermal Analysis Under review

Spatiotemporal Characterization and Short-Term Forecasting of Inlet Temperature in a GPU-Intensive Data Center

Inlet temperature is the air a server actually breathes, and in a GPU data center it differs from node to node. Using one month of measurements from 980 nodes, this study separates two questions that are usually blended together.

Question 1 · persistent Which nodes stay warm, month after month? Rack location, not power Question 2 · short-term What will a node's temperature do in the next 5–30 minutes? Its own recent temperature history

Findings

1Location, not power, sets which nodes run warm

Node-to-node differences in monthly mean inlet temperature were explained step by step. Power and job placement barely help; adding the node's position in the room, above all its height in the rack, explains most of the variation, and the pattern holds even for racks left out of fitting (R² 0.531).

2Recent history, not location, predicts the next few minutes

Forecasts started from persistence: assume the temperature stays where it is. A gradient-boosted model that learns from each node's last hour of inlet temperature beats it at every horizon.

Once recent temperatures are known, location and power add almost nothing: a node's location is already reflected in its current temperature. Power alone does no better than a constant guess.

Top 5 slots · sustained exposure

Warmest, up to about +2.4 °C above average, but changes least: 10-min forecast error 0.098 °C.

Bottom 5 slots · short-term change

Cooler, yet changes fastest: 10-min forecast error 0.182 °C, nearly twice the top.

For monitoring, this suggests two targets: use location to find nodes with sustained thermal exposure, and keep reliable, continuous inlet sensors to anticipate short-term changes.

Study details

  • Data: public operational dataset from a GPU-based HPC facility (Borghesi et al. 2023) · July 2022 · 980 nodes at ~1-min intervals · 411,348 job records
  • Methods: spline regression on power, job placement, and position with rack-level cross-validation · gradient-boosted trees forecasting the temperature change 5–30 min ahead
  • Scope: one month at one facility; associations, not causes. Cooling operation, airflow, and GPU utilization were not measured
  • Authors: Seungmin Lee, Juwan Ha, Yujin Kim, Jaeyoon Byun, Soolyeon Cho
