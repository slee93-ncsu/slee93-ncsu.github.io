Data Center Thermal Analysis Under review

Spatiotemporal Characterization and Short-Term Forecasting of Inlet Temperature in a GPU-Intensive Data Center

Each server node in a GPU data center takes in air at a different temperature. Using one month of data from 980 nodes, this study asks two separate questions: which nodes are always warmer, and how a node's temperature will change in the next few minutes.

Question 1 · long-term Which nodes are always warmer than others? Their location in the rack, not their power use Question 2 · short-term How will a node's temperature change in the next 5–30 minutes? Its own temperature over the past hour

Findings

1Where a node sits matters more than how much power it uses

Power use explains only 5% of the temperature differences between nodes. Adding each node's location in the room raises this to 60%, and height in the rack matters most. The result also holds for racks the model has never seen.

2A node's recent temperature is the best guide to the next few minutes

The baseline simply assumes the temperature stays the same. A model that uses each node's temperature over the past hour does better at every time horizon.

Once the recent temperature is known, location and power add almost nothing, because a node's location is already reflected in its current temperature. Power alone predicts no better than a fixed guess.

Takeaway

Top 5 slots · warmest, but steady

Up to about 2.4 °C warmer than average, but their temperature changes the least. 10-min forecast error: 0.098 °C.

Bottom 5 slots · cooler, but change fastest

Their temperature swings the most. 10-min forecast error: 0.182 °C, almost twice the top.

Monitoring therefore needs two approaches: use location to find the nodes that are always hot, and use reliable, continuous temperature sensors to catch short-term changes.

Study details

  • Data: public operating data from a GPU-based supercomputer (Borghesi et al. 2023) · July 2022 · 980 nodes measured about every minute · 411,348 job records
  • Methods: regression with smooth location terms, tested on held-out racks · gradient-boosted tree forecasts 5–30 minutes ahead
  • Scope: one month at one facility. The results show associations, not causes; cooling operation, airflow, and GPU utilization were not measured.
  • Authors: Seungmin Lee, Juwan Ha, Yujin Kim, Jaeyoon Byun, Soolyeon Cho
