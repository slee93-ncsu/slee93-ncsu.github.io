AI-Based HVAC Optimal Control Completed

EMS with an Optimal Control Algorithm for Energy-Efficient Commercial Buildings Using AI and Digital Twin Technology

KEIT · MOTIE 2021 – 2024 Inter Electric · Korea Univ. · NC State BETLab

At a manufacturing plant in South Carolina, artificial neural networks (ANNs) predict HVAC energy use, and an optimizer computes the setpoints that reduce energy while keeping indoor conditions within comfort limits. As the project's international partner, NC State BETLab carried out this U.S. demonstration.

Manufacturing plantSouth Carolina, USA · built 1998
Climate zone 3AWarm-humid; cooling-dominated
1,374.5 tonsCooling capacity: chillers, GMUs, RTUs, DLHPs
BACnet toolboxPyTorch + CasADi/IPOPT on an isolated network

Control system

BAS data

  • Space temperature & setpoint
  • Supply fan & capacity status
  • Discharge air temperature
  • Outdoor air damper, occupancy
  • Outdoor temperature & humidity, time
→

ANN models

Predict hourly heating and cooling energy from 10 selected inputs

PyTorch · ASHRAE Guideline 14: CV(RMSE) ≤ 15%, NMBE ±10%

→

Optimizer

Minimizes a cost function under comfort constraints at every time step

CasADi + IPOPT, with the ANN converted to a symbolic function

→

BAS

Receives the optimal setpoints and runs the HVAC units

BACnet + TCP/IP · isolated network

Input variables were chosen by correlation with heating or cooling energy (R² > 0.7, or |r| > 0.3 as a secondary criterion), with outdoor conditions and time added to capture weather and daily patterns.

Measured energy savings

Lesson learned: a January 2025 winter storm sharply raised prediction error, showing that model-predictive building control needs extreme-weather data in training.
