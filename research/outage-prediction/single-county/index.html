Power Outage Prediction

Single County Reviewed

Forecasting High-Impact, Low-Probability Power Outages with Temporal Fusion Transformers: A Case Study of Wake County

This study evaluates the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) for short-term outage prediction in Wake County, North Carolina, benchmarked against a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network.

The challenge

Outage data are long-tailed

Most outages affected fewer than several dozen customers, while the top 1% included events that impacted thousands to tens of thousands of customers. A quantile forecasting approach was therefore adopted in place of point forecasting, estimating the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles.

Findings

1TFT outperformed the LSTM across all evaluation metrics

TFT achieved an R² of 0.777 and reduced MAE by nearly 50 percent relative to the LSTM, with consistently lower Quantile RMSE across the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles. The LSTM yielded an R² close to zero and consistently underrepresented both the timing and magnitude of high-impact events.

2Atmospheric pressure and recent temporal context were dominant predictors

The variable selection network showed that atmospheric pressure (altimeter) kept a relatively stable and elevated importance, with a pronounced increase before outage events, suggesting it may serve as a leading indicator. The importance of the relative time index surged around sharp increases in outage magnitude.

Study details

  • Outage data: DOE EAGLE-I, Wake County, NC · 15-minute intervals · weather-related events only (NOAA, PNNL)
  • Weather data: ASOS meteorological observations and five composite variables · population and households as static covariates
  • Setup: summer months (June–October), 2018–2023 · tested on August 2023 · 2-day input, 6-hour forecast horizon
  • Limitations: extreme outages are rare in the training data, and grid topology and infrastructure condition were not included
